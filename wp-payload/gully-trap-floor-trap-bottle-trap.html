Gully trap vs floor trap vs bottle trap: which one goes where

A bathroom that smells of drain usually gets blamed on the pipe. Often the trap is the cause: the wrong one was fitted, or the water inside it has dried up. Here is what each trap does, where it goes, and what to write on the order.

26 Nov 2026 5 min read Hrivika export desk

Every drain in a house has a bend that stays full of water. That small pool is the only thing stopping sewer air from coming back into the room. The bend is called a trap, and there are four or five kinds on a normal site.

The names get mixed up easily, partly because one trap can go by two or three names in different shops. Get the name wrong on an order and the wrong part arrives. This guide sorts them out.

What is a gully trap?

A gully trap sits outside the building, at ground level, usually in a small brick chamber with a grating on top. India's building drainage code, IS 1742, describes it as the trap that collects waste water from the kitchen sink, wash basins, baths and rain-water pipes.

From the gully, the water goes on to an inspection chamber and then to the sewer. The toilet is not on that list. Toilet waste runs in its own soil pipe.

One rule from the same code is worth knowing. A rain-water pipe must never be joined to a soil pipe. Where rain water has to go into a sewer, it goes through a gully trap, and a gully that takes a rain-water pipe should have a screen and a silt catcher.

Floor trap and nahani trap: the indoor ones

A floor trap sits under the bathroom, balcony or kitchen floor. The grating on top (the jali) sits level with the tiles, and the shower and floor-wash water runs into it.

In India the same thing is often called a nahani trap. The name changes from shop to shop, but the job is the same: catch the floor water and hold a water seal under the floor. A multi floor trap has more than one inlet, so a basin or washing machine waste can join it under the floor.

IS 1742 also says the size of a floor trap depends on how much floor area it has to drain. A small bathroom and a large wash area should not get the same trap.

Bottle trap and P trap

A bottle trap goes under a wash basin or a sink, where you can see it. It is shaped like a small bottle, and the bottom unscrews, so a ring or a hair clog can be taken out without cutting anything.

A P trap is a bent pipe. Its outlet comes out sideways, into the wall. An S trap bends twice and its outlet goes down, into the floor. In our SWR range the P trap is a drain fitting for the soil and waste lines, in 3 inch, 4 inch and 5 × 4 inch.

The depth of water in the trap matters too. The UK's Approved Document H, for example, asks for a 75 mm water seal under a basin or sink. It also asks that every trap keeps at least 25 mm of water while the drains are in use.

That is why a floor trap in a bathroom nobody uses can start to smell. The water dries up and the seal is gone. Pour a bucket of water into it and the smell usually stops.

What to write on the order

Write the trap's name, the size in mm, the outlet size if it reduces, and whether you want the grating or top tile. Our nahani trap comes with or without the jali, and the floor trap comes with or without the top tile, so say which.

Common questions

What is the difference between a gully trap and a floor trap?

A gully trap sits outside the building and collects waste water from the kitchen, basins, baths and rain-water pipes. A floor trap sits under an indoor floor and takes the shower and floor water.

What is the purpose of a gully trap?

It holds a water seal so drain air cannot come back up, and it collects waste water before it goes on to the inspection chamber and the sewer.

Is a nahani trap the same as a floor trap?

Yes. Nahani trap is the name many Indian shops use for the indoor floor trap with a grating (jali) on top.

Why does my floor trap smell?

Usually the water in it has dried up because the drain is not used. Pour water into it to refill the seal, and check that the grating and trap are not blocked.

Why use a bottle trap instead of a P trap under a basin?

A bottle trap looks neater under an open basin, and its bottom unscrews, so it can be cleaned without taking the pipe apart.
