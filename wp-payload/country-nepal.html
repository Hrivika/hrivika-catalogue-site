Paperwork for Nepal

  • Your side. An EXIM code from the Department of Customs, registered through the Nepal National Single Window with your company registration and PAN. Your customs agent files the declaration in ASYCUDA World at the border.
  • Tax. VAT is 13%, charged on the CIF value plus customs duty. Duty rates can change with each budget, so confirm the duty for each HS code with your agent; our HS codes guide gives the first six digits.
  • Payment. Under Nepal Rastra Bank rules, imports from India are normally settled in Indian rupees. Your bank will tell you whether your order should go by letter of credit or another route.
  • Our side. The commercial invoice, packing list and transport documents. We can also supply a certificate of origin; ask your customs agent whether your consignment needs one, and tell us if you plan to claim a tariff concession. Our export documents pack note covers each document.
  • Tenders. If a tender names a particular standard, send us the tender wording before you order, and the desk confirms what it can supply against it.

Across the border: Birgunj, Biratnagar and the other crossings

Indian goods reach Nepal by road, or by rail for containers. Birgunj, opposite Raxaul, handled 47% of Nepal's imports in FY 2082/83, ahead of Bhairahawa (14.5%) and Biratnagar (11.5%). Birgunj has an integrated check post for trucks and the Sirsiya dry port for container trains. Since July 2026, container trains from Kolkata have also run to Biratnagar through Jogbani, and Biratnagar and Nepalgunj have integrated check posts of their own.

We quote FOB or CIF, or delivered to the border customs office you clear through, for example Birgunj or Biratnagar. Tell us which you want, because on a delivered price the freight to that point is part of the price. FOB and CIF are sea terms; for the road leg to the border, the contract names a road term and the border point, for example CPT Birgunj. Our FOB vs CIF note explains the difference.

Questions Nepali buyers ask

Can you deliver to Birgunj or Biratnagar?

Yes. We quote delivered to the border customs office you clear through, such as Birgunj or Biratnagar, or FOB or CIF. Name the customs office on your enquiry.

Which is better, PVC or HDPE?

It depends on the ground and the water. HDPE suits buried mains, long runs and uneven or shifting ground, because it bends and comes in coils. uPVC suits straight, accessible cold-water lines, where it is cheaper. For hot water inside a building, use CPVC rather than either of these.

What are the diameters of HDPE pipes in millimetres?

HDPE pipe is named by its outside diameter in millimetres. Our range runs through 26 sizes from 20 mm to 800 mm: 20, 25, 32, 40, 50, 63, 75, 90 and 110 mm, then 125 mm upwards. In the trade, 20 mm is often called ½ inch, 25 mm ¾ inch, 32 mm 1 inch and 63 mm 2 inch. That is shorthand, and the sizes do not convert exactly.

Which pipe is costly, PVC or HDPE?

HDPE is usually the more expensive pipe per metre at a similar pressure class. The gap narrows on a long buried line, where HDPE coils save joints, fittings and labour, so compare the cost of the whole installed line.

Which brand of CPVC pipe is the best in Nepal?

Start with what is printed on the pipe. The legend should name the standard, the size and the SDR, and the fittings and solvent cement must be CPVC, not uPVC. A lower SDR means a thicker wall and a higher pressure rating. Our CPVC comes in SDR 9, 11 and 13.5, for hot and cold water up to 93 °C.

Which pipe is stronger, PVC or uPVC?

For pressure pipe they are normally the same material. "PVC pipe" in plumbing almost always means unplasticised PVC, which is uPVC. Plasticised PVC is the soft material of garden hose. Compare pipes by their pressure class.
