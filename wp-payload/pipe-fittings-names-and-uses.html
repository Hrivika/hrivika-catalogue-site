Pipe fittings names and uses: decoding an Indian fittings list

An Indian fittings price list reads like code. MTA (B) 1 × ½. FT tee. R.M.T.A. 75 × 50. Tank connector MT/SOC. Sch 11. Each line is exact to the plumber who wrote it.

30 Oct 2026 13 min read Hrivika export desk

To a buyer in another country, each of those lines is a guess.

The fitting is rarely the problem. The problem is a ½-inch CPVC elbow arriving for a ½-inch uPVC line. Or a plastic thread where a brass valve has to screw in. Those mistakes surface on site, after the container is unpacked.

This guide gives pipe fittings names and uses in plain English, with photos from our catalogue. It translates the Indian trade codes, shows which fitting exists in which pipe system, and ends with how to write each fitting on a purchase order. Every fitting links to its catalogue page.

The short answer: five jobs, three labels

Every pipe fitting does one of five jobs:

  • Turn the line: elbow, shoe, bend
  • Branch it: tee, cross tee, Y
  • Join pipe, or step down a size: coupler, reducer, bush, union
  • Connect to a thread: MTA, FTA, threaded elbow or tee, nipple, tank connector
  • Close the end: end cap, plug

An Indian list then adds three labels to each name. Which pipe system it belongs to. What each end is: a plain socket, a male thread or a female thread. And the size of each end. Get those three right and the name looks after itself.

Two codes cause most of the trouble.

"Sch 11" on CPVC is not a schedule. Some Indian CPVC price lists label pipe "Schedule 11" or "Sch 11". It means SDR 11 to IS 15778, the CTS series. It has nothing to do with Schedule 40 or 80. Order CPVC by SDR, and say CTS or IPS.

(B) or (P) is not a price choice. A metal male thread screwed into a plastic female thread can split the plastic as it is tightened. That is why brass-insert fittings exist. Use (B) wherever a brass tap, valve, pump or meter connects. Plastic threads are fine plastic-to-plastic.

A few gaps are worth knowing before you build a list:

  • Ys appear in the drainage and irrigation ranges. Indoor pressure plumbing uses tees.
  • On our list, hex nipples appear only in CPVC.
  • The HDPE compression range covers couplers, elbows, tees, end caps, threaded adaptors and saddles. On our list it has no 45° elbow or reducer.
  • The PPR range has the widest choice of brass and threaded unions.

Where two systems meet: transition fittings

Two different pipes almost always meet through a thread. Fit an MTA on one side and an FTA, or a threaded valve, on the other. That works between uPVC, CPVC, PPR, HDPE and metal.

  • PPR to metal: a brass union or a threaded union. You can undo it later for a meter or pump.
  • CPVC CTS to Schedule (IPS) fittings: a transition bush. US-standard bushings are an IPS spigot × a CTS socket. Confirm which way round ours is on the proforma.
  • Flanged pumps and valves: a flange adaptor, listed in the fire-protection range.

Check the thread standard as well as the size. Indian plumbing fittings are commonly sold with BSP (British pipe) threads, but do not assume it: ask which thread standard the fittings carry. Much American equipment uses NPT. The two look alike and can be screwed together, but they do not seal reliably. If your valves are NPT, write it on the order and ask for confirmation in writing.

Write the purchase order line so nobody has to guess

Write one line per fitting, in this order:

  1. Pipe system and standard: "CPVC, CTS, SDR 11, IS 15778"
  2. Fitting name, plus the code you know: "MTA (male threaded adaptor)"
  3. Size, pipe end first: "25 × 15 mm (1 × ½ in)"
  4. Thread material and standard: "brass insert, BSP"
  5. Quantity in pieces

Common questions

What are the different types of pipe fittings and their names?

Pipe fittings are grouped by job. Elbows, shoes and bends turn the line. Tees, cross tees and Ys branch it. Couplers, reducers, bushes and unions join pipe. MTAs, FTAs, nipples and tank connectors connect to threads. End caps and plugs close it.

How many types of pipe fittings are there?

There is no fixed number. Most fittings fall into five jobs: change direction, branch, join or change size, connect to a thread, and close the end. Each job then has a version for each pipe system.

What is MTA and FTA in plumbing?

MTA is a male threaded adaptor: a socket for the pipe at one end and an external thread at the other. FTA is a female threaded adaptor, with an internal thread. (B) after either means a brass thread insert; (P) means a plastic thread.

What is the difference between a reducer and a bush?

A reducer has a socket at each end and joins a larger pipe straight to a smaller one. A bush pushes into the socket of a larger fitting and takes a smaller pipe inside it. Use a bush to step down at a tee or elbow without adding a reducer.

What is the difference between an elbow and a bend?

An elbow makes a tight turn and is the usual fitting on pressure lines. A bend makes a wider, gentler turn. It is used in drainage, where waste has to flow round the corner without catching.

Can CPVC fittings be used on uPVC pipe?

No. Most CPVC plumbing pipe in India is CTS-sized, so a ½-inch CPVC fitting takes a 15.9 mm pipe, while ½-inch Schedule uPVC is 21.3 mm. The two also need different solvent cements. Join them through a threaded adaptor instead.
