UPVC vs CPVC vs PPR vs HDPE: which pipe for which job

Most pipe failures are not caused by bad pipe. They are caused by the right pipe used in the wrong place.

2 Aug 2026 13 min read Hrivika export desk

A hot line run in uPVC softens. A CPVC riser sealed with the wrong thread paste cracks later. A buried main in rigid pipe splits when the ground settles. None of these is a manufacturing fault. Each is a specification mistake.

This guide starts with uPVC vs CPVC, the choice most buyers face, then adds PPR and HDPE. Every figure carries the standard behind it and the temperature it applies at.

The short answer

  • uPVC — cold water under pressure, irrigation, borewell casing. Rigid, solvent-cemented, the lowest cost per metre. Not for hot water.
  • CPVC — hot and cold water inside buildings. Solvent-cemented with its own CPVC cement. Rated to 82 °C under the Indian standard.
  • PPR — hot and cold water inside buildings, joined by heat fusion instead of cement.
  • HDPE — buried mains, long runs, irrigation. Flexible, supplied in coils, heat-fused. Cold water only.
Full forms

uPVC: unplasticised polyvinyl chloride. CPVC: chlorinated polyvinyl chloride. PPR: polypropylene random copolymer. HDPE: high-density polyethylene.

uPVC vs CPVC

Verdict: uPVC is the cold-water pipe. CPVC is the hot-and-cold pipe. If any line runs hot, that line is CPVC or PPR, and uPVC and CPVC do not share fittings.

CPVC is PVC chlorinated a second time: about 67% chlorine by weight in commercial resins, against 57% in PVC. The extra chlorine raises the softening point. uPVC pressure pipe must reach a Vicat softening temperature of at least 80 °C; IS 15778 requires CPVC to reach 110 °C.

Start with temperature

IS 4985 rates uPVC pressure pipe at 27 °C, with reduced pressure above that. Its 2000 edition recommends the pipe for water from 1 °C to 45 °C, and ISO 1452 also covers PVC-U pipe only up to 45 °C. The 60 °C you often read is the American ceiling for PVC (140 °F), by which point most of the pressure rating is gone.

IS 15778 covers CPVC for water service "up to and including 82 °C". Many brands advertise 93 °C. That is a material ceiling from makers' literature, not the range the Indian standard rates. Design hot lines at 82 °C or below.

Pressure falls as CPVC heats

IS 15778 rates every CPVC pipe at two temperatures:

At 82 °C the pipe holds about a quarter of its cold rating. ASTM D2846, the American standard for the same CTS pipe, gives the same picture: 400 psi at 23 °C, 100 psi at 82 °C. Size from the hot column.

SDR means standard dimension ratio: the outside diameter divided by the wall thickness. A lower SDR means a thicker wall and more pressure.

The sizes do not cross over

uPVC to IS 4985 is sized by outside diameter (OD) in millimetres. A 25 mm pipe is 25 mm across. Schedule uPVC to ASTM D1785 is sized in inches: 1 inch Schedule 40 is 33.4 mm OD. CPVC to IS 15778 uses copper tube size from ½" to 2": ¾" CPVC is 22.2 mm OD, and 1" is 28.6 mm.

So a 25 mm uPVC pipe, a 1 inch Schedule 40 pipe and a 1 inch CPVC pipe are three different diameters. None will seat in another's fitting. The PVC pipe size chart sets out what each system calls the same size.

Each needs its own cement

CPVC cement is made to ASTM F493. PVC cement is made to ASTM D2564. They are not substitutes. Buy the cement and primer with the pipe, for the same system.

Drinking water and cost

IS 4985 is written for uPVC pipe for potable water supply. IS 15778 covers CPVC for potable hot and cold water. Third-party approvals such as NSF/ANSI 61 belong to a brand and a product, not to the material. Ask for the certificate on the line you are buying.

CPVC costs more per metre than uPVC of the same size, because the resin goes through an extra chlorination step.

Is uPVC the same as PVC?

In the pipe trade, yes. Rigid PVC pressure pipe is unplasticised PVC. That is what the "u" means, and it is what IS 4985 and ISO 1452 cover. When a bill of quantities says "PVC pipe" for a water main, it means uPVC.

The difference is real elsewhere. Flexible PVC — garden hose, layflat hose — has plasticisers added so it bends. It is a different material for a different job. Window frames are also sold as "uPVC", which is why searches for the two terms mix pipes with windows.

On site, rigid pipe will not bend by hand, and its print line names a pipe standard such as IS 4985 or ASTM D1785. And since rigid "PVC" and "uPVC" pipe are the same material, neither costs more than the other.

CPVC vs PPR

Verdict: both carry hot and cold water. CPVC has its pressure at 82 °C printed in the Indian standard and joins with cement and no power tools. PPR joins by heat fusion and has no cement to get wrong. The crew, the water and the building decide — not the material.

Compare pressure at your design temperature

PPR is sold as SDR 11 (PN 10), SDR 7.4 (PN 16) and SDR 6 (PN 20). IS 15801 designates the pipe by SDR. The PN label is trade shorthand for water at 20 °C, not the pressure the pipe holds at 70 °C.

IS 15801 gives an informative table of allowable pressure by temperature, derived from DIN 8077. Ask for the figure at your design temperature and set it against CPVC's 82 °C rating. Never compare PN 20 PPR with SDR 11 CPVC on the headline numbers.

The sizes do not match

¾" CPVC is 22.2 mm outside. PPR's nearest sizes are 20 mm and 25 mm. There is no straight swap: changing systems needs a transition fitting, and converting a design means comparing bores, not labels.

What each camp says about the other

  • PPR and hot chlorinated water. Polypropylene can lose its protective antioxidants in hot, chlorinated water and turn brittle. The American standard ASTM F2389 now grades polypropylene systems in chlorine classes 0, 1, 3 and 5. Ask which class the pipe carries.
  • CPVC and chemicals on site. Some thread sealants, firestop products and other site chemicals can crack CPVC. Lubrizol, which makes CPVC compound, runs a programme that tests these products for compatibility. Use only listed products.
  • Fire. CPVC suppliers point out that CPVC does not keep burning in air, while PPR does. Where flame and smoke rules apply, such as in ceiling voids, check what your local code accepts.

PPR also expands more than CPVC as it heats, so long hot runs need loops, offsets or the fibre-reinforced grade.

Which is better, PPR or uPVC? They do different jobs. uPVC is cold-water pipe. PPR handles hot and cold. For a cold main where cost matters, use uPVC. For a hot line, use PPR or CPVC.

HDPE vs PVC

Verdict: HDPE for buried mains, long runs and moving ground. uPVC for cold supply in stable trenches and above ground, where its stiffness and lower cost help.

Flexible coils against rigid lengths

HDPE bends. Up to about 110 mm it comes in coils, so a long run has very few joints. A good butt-fused or electrofused joint is as strong as the pipe. That is why HDPE goes under roads by directional drilling and across uneven ground.

uPVC comes in rigid straight lengths, joined with solvent cement or rubber-ring sockets. It holds its line in a trench and sags less above ground.

They are rated differently

uPVC to IS 4985 is sold by class, rated at 27 °C:

The class number is a code, not a pressure. Class 4 is the 8-bar pipe.

HDPE is rated by material grade and SDR together. Under ISO 4427, PE 100 at SDR 11 is PN 16, and at SDR 17 it is PN 10, both at 20 °C. The same SDR in PE 80 gives a lower rating. On a pumped line, choose one SDR thicker than the calculation says, because pump shutdown creates surge.

Same outside diameter, different pipe

A 110 mm HDPE pipe and a 110 mm uPVC pipe share an outside diameter, and nothing else. Wall thickness follows the class or SDR, and polyethylene cannot be solvent-cemented. Joining the two needs a mechanical transition, such as a flanged stub end or a coupling made for both materials.

What HDPE costs you

  • It needs fusion equipment and a trained crew. A badly fused joint is the usual failure point.
  • It is a cold-water pipe. Its rating falls as the water warms.
  • It moves more with temperature than PVC, so long surface runs snake.
  • Above ground it needs closer supports than rigid pipe.
  • In small sizes it usually costs more per metre than uPVC of the same pressure.

The best HDPE pipe for a job is not a brand. It is the right grade and SDR to the right standard: PE 100 to IS 4984 or ISO 4427, with the SDR chosen for working pressure plus surge.

Where SWR fits

SWR is uPVC soil, waste and rainwater pipe, made to IS 13592 with fittings to IS 14735. It moves waste by gravity. It is not a pressure pipe and must never be used as one.

Type A has the thinner wall and goes on vents and rainwater downtakes. Type B has the thicker wall and goes on soil and waste stacks. Do not swap Type B for Type A to save money. On vertical stacks, use ring-fit joints so the pipe can move.

In hot climates, read the reference temperature

Every rating is quoted at a reference temperature: 20 °C under ISO, 27 °C under IS 4985 and IS 15778, 23 °C under ASTM. A line clipped to a sunlit roof in the Gulf sits far above all of them. Shade or insulate exposed runs, and size from the derated figure.

How to tell which pipe you have

Read the standard on the print line before anything else: IS 4985 or ASTM D1785 is uPVC, IS 15778, ASTM D2846 or ASTM F441 is CPVC, IS 15801 is PPR, IS 4984 or ISO 4427 is HDPE, and IS 13592 is SWR drainage pipe. Colour is only a hint, so if you are unsure, send a photo of the print line before you order.

What to put on the RFQ

Name the material and standard, the size system, the class, SDR or schedule, and the design temperature of any hot line — "uPVC to IS 4985, Class 4", not "PVC pipe". What to take away

  • Temperature decides first. uPVC and HDPE are cold-water pipes. CPVC is rated to 82 °C under IS 15778. PPR goes to 95 °C under IS 15801, at much-reduced pressure.
  • Compare pressure at your working temperature, never by the headline class.
  • The four systems use different sizes and joints. Nothing crosses over without a transition fitting.
  • Trust the standard on the print line before the colour.

Common questions

Which is better, UPVC or CPVC?

Neither is better overall. uPVC is the right pipe for cold water under pressure, and it costs less. CPVC is the right pipe for hot water: IS 15778 rates it to 82 °C, while uPVC pressure pipe is limited to 45 °C.

Is UPVC good for hot water?

No. uPVC pressure pipe is a cold-water pipe: it is rated at 27 °C, loses pressure rating as it warms, and is limited to 45 °C. Use CPVC or PPR for hot lines.

Is UPVC pipe safe for drinking water?

uPVC made to IS 4985 is designed for potable water supply. Third-party approvals such as NSF/ANSI 61 are held by specific brands and products, so ask for the certificate on the pipe you buy.

Which is costly, CPVC or UPVC?

CPVC costs more per metre than uPVC of the same size, because its resin goes through an extra chlorination step. The gap varies by size and class, so compare quoted prices for your actual line items.

Is PVC the same as uPVC?

For rigid pipe, yes. Rigid PVC pressure pipe is unplasticised PVC (uPVC or PVC-U), as covered by IS 4985 and ISO 1452. Flexible PVC, used in hoses, has plasticisers added and is a different material.

Which is better, PPR or CPVC?

Neither is better overall. CPVC has its 82 °C pressure rating printed in IS 15778 and joins with cement, no power needed. PPR joins by heat fusion and has no cement to get wrong. Choose by design temperature, crew skills and water chemistry.
