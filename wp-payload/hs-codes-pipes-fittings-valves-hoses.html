HS codes for PVC pipes, fittings, valves and hoses: an importer's guide

A wrong HS code rarely shows up at the loading port. It shows up at yours, when customs reclassifies the goods, reworks the duty and holds the container until the paperwork agrees.

30 Oct 2026 12 min read Hrivika export desk

The short answer: the HS code for PVC pipe is 3917.23, rigid tubes, pipes and hoses of polymers of vinyl chloride. Plastic fittings are 3917.40. Most valves and taps, brass or PVC, are 8481.80. Check valves are 8481.30.

Those six digits are the same in India, the UAE and Kenya. The digits after them are not. Below are the six-digit codes for each product family, then the national lines for all three, checked against the official tariffs on 29 October 2026.

Two items in a typical order sit outside these headings. Y-strainers are filters, not valves. Brass compression fittings are copper-alloy pipe fittings, 7412.20.

How the digits work

The first six digits are the Harmonized System (HS), maintained by the World Customs Organization. The current edition is HS 2022; HS 2028 takes over on 1 January 2028. Each country then adds its own digits.

Two things follow from this.

First, the Indian code on our shipping bill is for Indian export customs. Your customs reads its own tariff. The first six digits should match on both sides. The rest will not, and that is normal.

Second, the code you declare has to agree with your commercial invoice and packing list. Our export documents guide shows how those papers line up.

The GCC adopted a 12-digit integrated tariff from January 2025, and Dubai Customs has been phasing the 12-digit codes in since August 2025, with the old 8-digit lines running alongside during the transition. Ask your broker which length your declaration needs today.

Plastic pipe: the polymer decides the code

Heading 3917 covers plastic tubes, pipes, hoses and their fittings. For rigid pipe, the subheading follows the plastic. Size, pressure class and colour do not change it. A 20 mm pipe and a 315 mm pipe share a code.

The HS code for PVC pipe: 3917.23

uPVC pressure pipe, SWR drainage pipe and column pipe are all rigid PVC, so all 3917.23.

India splits this into 3917 23 10 (seamless tubes) and 3917 23 90 (other). The tariff does not define "seamless". Which line goes on the shipping bill is a call for the Indian exporter's customs broker.

One widely quoted Indian HSN site gives 3917 21 90 for PVC pipe. That line sits under 3917.21, polymers of ethylene. It is the wrong plastic.

CPVC pipe: no settled answer

CPVC is PVC chlorinated after it is made. In tariff terms that is a chemically modified polymer, and Chapter 39's subheading notes send chemically modified polymers to the "other" line where one exists. US Customs, for example, classifies CPVC resin under 3904.90, the "other" line, not with plain PVC resin.

Whether the same logic moves CPVC pipe from 3917.23 to 3917.29 ("of other plastics") is not settled. Several online tariff tools list CPVC under 3917.23. We found no customs ruling on CPVC pipe either way. Before a first CPVC shipment, ask your broker for a written view.

HDPE pipe: 3917.21, not 3901.20

HDPE pipe is 3917.21. In India it is 3917 21 10, "tubes of polyethylene".

You will see 3901.20 quoted for HDPE pipe. That code covers polyethylene in primary forms, specific gravity 0.94 or more. It is the resin, not the pipe. PE80 or PE100 grade does not change the pipe code either.

PPR pipe: 3917.22

PP-R is a random copolymer in which propylene is the main monomer. Chapter 39 classifies a copolymer with the monomer that predominates by weight. So PPR pipe is 3917.22, the same as plain PP pipe.

Fittings: 3917.40 if plastic

Every plastic fitting is 3917.40: elbows, tees, sockets, reducers, unions, MTAs, FTAs, and the compression fittings used on HDPE.

Metal fittings leave Chapter 39. Brass and other copper-alloy fittings go to 7412.20, and iron or steel fittings to heading 7307.

3917.31 is only for hose with a minimum burst pressure of 27.6 MPa (about 276 bar). PVC suction, garden and layflat hoses are normally rated well below that.

A layflat hose built as a woven textile jacket with a plastic lining, like a fire hose, can fall under heading 5909, textile hosepiping, instead. Check how yours is made.

Garden hose is where customs authorities themselves disagree. In 2016 US Customs placed a polyester-braided PVC garden hose under 3917.39. In 2023 UK customs issued a ruling placing a braided PVC garden hose under 3917.23, the rigid PVC line. If two customs services can read a hose two ways, so can your port.

In India, PVC hose most likely takes the "addition polymerisation products" lines: 3917 39 20 if reinforced, 3917 32 20 if plain. PVC is an addition polymer. That is our reading of the tariff text, so confirm the line with your customs broker.

Valves: the job decides the code, not the material

Heading 8481 covers taps, cocks and valves for pipes and tanks. Its subheadings sort valves by what they do.

Material does not change the six digits. A bronze ball valve, a forged brass one and a PVC one are all 8481.80. Plastic valves do not stay in the plastics chapter: Chapter 39's notes exclude articles of Section XVI, the machinery section that holds heading 8481.

HS code for ball valves: 8481.80

A complete ball valve is 8481.80, whether brass, bronze, stainless steel or PVC. You will see PVC ball valves quoted under 8481.90. That subheading is for parts, and a finished valve is not a part.

Check valves and foot valves: 8481.30

Swing, lift and vertical check valves are 8481.30. A foot valve is a check valve with a strainer, fitted at the bottom of a suction line, so it usually goes to 8481.30 as well. Confirm it with your broker.

Gate, globe and butterfly valves: 8481.80

None of these has its own subheading, so all three fall to 8481.80.

Y-strainers are not valves

A Y-strainer traps dirt. It does not open or shut the line. US Customs classifies Y-strainers as filtering apparatus for liquids under heading 8421, not 8481. Ask your broker which 8421 line fits your use.

Taps, bib cocks and health faucets: 8481.80, even in plastic

A PVC tap is still a tap, so the same Chapter 39 exclusion sends it to 8481.80, like a brass one. Trade data shows plastic taps declared under three other codes: 3926.90 (other articles of plastics), 3917.39 (hose) and 3917.40 (fittings). Those records show what was declared, not what was right.

The national lines then split further. The UAE has separate 12-digit lines for a water faucet and for a shattaf, or health faucet. India splits taps by material: iron or steel taps are 8481 80 10, brass and other non-ferrous taps 8481 80 20, and plastic taps fall to 8481 80 90.

Plastic sanitary ware has its own heading, 3922. Toilet seat covers are 3922.20 and flushing cisterns 3922.90.

Kenya's lines add two zeros to the HS code because the EAC does not split these subheadings further. India's valve split is the hard one. It separates taps and cocks by metal, "industrial valves", and "other", and the tariff defines none of those terms.

We have left duty rates out on purpose. They change by notification and by trade agreement, and a wrong rate is worse than none. Your customs broker will confirm the rate for your line and your origin.

Why the codes online disagree

Many pages that rank for these searches are built on shipment databases. Those record what an importer declared, not what customs accepted. That is how one plastic tap ends up with four codes, and how a polyethylene line, a resin code and a parts code get quoted for PVC pipe, HDPE pipe and ball valves.

Two more traps. The same eight digits can mean different things in India, the EU and the US, so a description borrowed from one tariff does not fit another's number. And a 12-digit "GCC code" for PVC pipe that circulates online does not appear in the UAE's published tariff.

One container, many codes

A mixed container of pipe, fittings, hoses and valves carries several HS codes. Give each product family its own invoice line with its own code. "PVC pipes and fittings" on one line under one code is the kind of entry that invites a reclassification. Our mixed containers guide covers how we build the list.

The HS code also says nothing about conformity. Kenya's KEBS PVoC certificate is a separate requirement, covered in our guide to importing PVC pipes from India. And under FOB or CIF terms, import duty is paid by you at destination; see FOB vs CIF.

Before you book

  1. Describe each line by material and construction: "PVC suction hose, PVC helix", not "hose".
  2. Ask your customs broker for the full national code for each line. For CPVC pipe, garden hose and foot valves, get the answer in writing.
  3. For a line you will import often, ask whether your customs issues advance rulings.
  4. Send us the list: product, size, class or PN, material, quantity, destination port, and your broker's code for each line.

Your broker makes the final classification. Our job is to make sure our invoice and packing list carry the same first six digits.

Common questions

What is the HS code for PVC pipe?

3917.23: rigid tubes, pipes and hoses of polymers of vinyl chloride. It covers uPVC pressure, SWR and column pipe. Countries add their own digits: India uses 3917 23 10 or 3917 23 90, Kenya 3917.23.00 and the UAE 3917 23 00 00 02.

What is the HS code for PVC pipe fittings?

3917.40, fittings of plastics. Elbows, tees, sockets, reducers, unions, MTAs and FTAs all go here. Brass fittings are classified separately, under 7412.20.

What is the HS code for a ball valve?

8481.80, whatever it is made of: brass, bronze, stainless steel or PVC. 8481.90 is for valve parts, not complete valves.

What is the 8-digit HSN code for HDPE pipe?

In India, 3917 21 10, "tubes of polyethylene". Size does not change it, so a 32 mm pipe and a 110 mm pipe share the code. HDPE resin is a different heading, 3901.

What is the HS code for PVC taps?

8481.80. A tap stays in heading 8481 even when it is plastic, because Chapter 39 excludes articles of Section XVI. In India, plastic taps take 8481 80 90.

What is HSN code 84818090?

It is India's "other" line under 8481.80, for taps, cocks and valves not named in the other 8481 80 lines, plastic taps among them. Metal taps have their own lines: 8481 80 10 for iron or steel and 8481 80 20 for non-ferrous metal.
