MTA vs FTA in plumbing: which threaded adapter goes where

A tap has a thread on its tail, a ball valve has threads inside its ends, and your pipe has no thread at all. Two small adapters sit between them, and a price list gives you three letters to tell them apart.

23 Nov 2026 8 min read Hrivika export desk

MTA stands for male threaded adapter and FTA for female threaded adapter. Indian price lists often spell it "adaptor". Both have a plain socket at one end, where your pipe goes in with solvent cement (or by heat fusion, on PPR). The other end is threaded. On an MTA the thread is on the outside. On an FTA it is cut on the inside.

That one difference decides which you need. A male thread screws into a female thread, so you pick the adapter that is the opposite of whatever sits at the other end of the joint.

How to tell them apart in a few seconds

Hold the fitting with the socket away from you and look at the threaded end.

  • If you can run a finger along the thread on the outside, it is an MTA.
  • If the outside is smooth and the thread is cut into the bore, it is an FTA.
  • If the thread sits in a yellow metal ring, it is a brass-insert version, written MTA (B) or FTA (B). An all-plastic one is written MTA (P) or FTA (P).

In a bathroom you usually meet both. The tap points in the wall are female, so the taps can screw in, and the valve that isolates the line takes an MTA on each side.

Brass thread or plastic thread

The (B) or (P) after the name tells you what the thread is cut in, and that matters more than it looks.

Pipe threads are tapered, so they wedge tighter with every turn. On a female fitting that wedge pushes the wall outwards. A metal tail driven hard into a plastic female thread can split it. Spears, an American maker of plastic fittings, names over-tightening as the most common cause of threaded joint failure for this reason (its installation note). A brass insert takes that load instead of the plastic.

  • A metal male thread going into your adapter (a tap, an angle valve, a GI pipe end): use the FTA (B).
  • Your MTA going into a metal female thread (a brass valve, a GI socket): plastic male into metal female is the arrangement fitting makers recommend, so an MTA (P) works on cold lines. On hot lines use the MTA (B). Charlotte Pipe, an American CPVC maker, warns that its all-plastic male adapters can fail on hot water and specifies brass-threaded adapters there.
  • Plastic into plastic, such as an MTA into a PVC valve with plastic threads: the all-plastic version is fine. Just don't over-tighten it.

BSP, NPT and what MPT means

Two thread systems are in everyday use, and they look almost the same.

BSP is the British standard pipe thread, used on taps, valves and GI pipe made to British and Indian practice. NPT is the American one. The thread angles differ: 55 degrees on BSP, 60 on NPT. At ½ and ¾ inch both have 14 threads per inch, so a BSP fitting will start onto an NPT one and then refuse to seal. At 1 inch even the pitch differs: 11 threads per inch on BSP, 11½ on NPT.

MPT and FPT are American shorthand for male pipe thread and female pipe thread, normally cut to NPT. So an "MPT adapter" in a US catalogue is an MTA with an NPT thread. "MPT vs NPT" is not a choice between two threads: MPT tells you the gender, NPT the standard.

Plastic threaded adapters are made both ways. If your taps or valves come from another source, ask for the thread to be stated on the quote, BSP or NPT, before the order is placed.

MTA and FTA in each pipe system

  • uPVC. Solvent-cement socket, with a plastic or brass thread. Our uPVC MTA (P) runs from 15 to 200 mm and the uPVC FTA (B) from 15 to 100 mm.
  • CPVC. Joined with CPVC cement, not PVC cement. MTA and FTA come in (P) and (B), from ½ to 2 inch (15 to 50 mm), including reducing sizes such as ¾ × ½.
  • PPR. Heat-fusion socket. The threaded joints carry a brass insert, sold as male and female threaded joints, and the trade still calls them MTA and FTA.
  • HDPE. Joined by fusion or with compression fittings, so its threaded adapters are compression fittings with a male or female end, in plastic or brass.

How the sizes are written

One size means the socket and the thread match: an FTA ½ takes ½-inch pipe and has a ½-inch thread. A reducing adapter carries two sizes, pipe first and thread second. Our CPVC FTA (B) list runs ½, ¾, ¾ × ½, 1, 1 × ½, 1 × ¾, 1¼, 1½ and 2. The ¾ × ½ takes ¾-inch CPVC pipe and has a ½-inch female thread, which is what a ½-inch tap needs on a ¾-inch line.

The millimetre names (15, 20 and 25 mm for ½, ¾ and 1 inch CPVC) are labels, not the outside diameter. A ½-inch CPVC pipe measures 15.9 mm across. The real dimensions are in our PVC pipe size chart and CPVC size chart.

Fitting one without splitting it

  1. Where you can, screw the adapter into the metal part first and cement the pipe into it afterwards, so the turning does not twist a fresh solvent joint.
  2. Wrap the male thread with PTFE tape, or use a thread sealant that its maker clears for your plastic. Spears warns that some joint compounds and PTFE pastes contain substances that can make thermoplastics crack.
  3. Screw the joint together by hand until it is finger tight.
  4. Finish with a strap wrench, one to two turns past finger tight at most. A pipe wrench chews plastic.

Common questions

What is the full form of MTA in plumbing?

Male threaded adapter, often spelt "adaptor" in India. It joins a pipe, through a plain socket, to a fitting with a female thread, such as a ball valve or a GI socket.

What is the full form of FTA in plumbing?

Female threaded adapter. It has a socket for the pipe at one end and an internal thread at the other, so a tap, an angle valve or a GI pipe end with a male thread screws into it.

What is the difference between MTA and FTA?

Where the thread is. An MTA's thread is on the outside and screws into something; an FTA's thread is inside and something screws into it.

What do (B) and (P) mean after MTA or FTA?

What the thread is made of: (B) is a brass insert, (P) is plastic. Use (B) wherever a metal male thread goes into the adapter, and on hot water lines.

What is MPT vs NPT?

MPT means male pipe thread. NPT is the American thread standard it is usually cut to. An MPT adapter is the American name for an MTA with an NPT thread. Fittings made to British and Indian practice use BSP instead.

Can I screw a brass tap into a plastic FTA?

Into an FTA (B), yes. An all-plastic FTA can split as the tapered metal thread tightens, so use the brass-insert version for taps.

Which is better, CPVC or uPVC?

For hot water, CPVC; uPVC is for cold lines. Our uPVC vs CPVC comparison goes through temperature, pressure and sizes.
