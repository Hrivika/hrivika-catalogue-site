PVC pipe size chart — mm to inch, nominal bore and outside diameter

A buyer orders one inch CPVC and one inch PVC for the same job. Both arrive on time. Neither set of fittings goes onto the other pipe.

4 Aug 2026 10 min read Hrivika export desk

Nothing was mis-supplied. Both really are one inch. One measures 28.6 mm across the outside and the other 33.4 mm.

Sizing is the most common ordering error in this trade, and it is almost never carelessness. It is that four sizing conventions are in use at the same time, and three of them use the word "inch" to mean different things.

The short answer

Order by outside diameter in millimetres, and name the standard. An order for "110 mm uPVC to IS 4985, Class 4" cannot be misread. An order for "4 inch PVC" can be filled five different ways, and at least three of them will not mate with your fittings.

Why the systems disagree

There are three ways to name a pipe, and each answers a different question.

Outside diameter systems name the outside of the pipe. All the plastics standards used in India work this way. The OD is fixed for a given size, and as the pressure class goes up the wall thickens inwards — so a Class 6 pipe has the same outside as a Class 2 pipe and a noticeably smaller bore. This is why fittings interchange across classes and why flow does not.

Nominal bore systems name an approximate inside dimension. Steel and GI work this way, which is why a "1 inch" GI pipe measures nothing like 1 inch anywhere on it.

Copper Tube Size (CTS) is the third and the one that catches people. The OD is the nominal size plus one eighth of an inch. A ½" CTS pipe is 0.625 inch across the outside — 15.9 mm. Most Indian CPVC plumbing pipe is CTS.

Pressure pipe for potable water is sold by outside diameter in millimetres. There is no inch size in the standard. IS 4985 covers 16 mm to 630 mm; 20 mm to 315 mm is what is stocked and exported in practice.

Do not convert a Class number into a PN number

Many export specifications are written to ISO 1452 rather than IS 4985, and the two are not interchangeable by arithmetic. IS 4985 states its class pressures at 27°C. ISO 1452-2 is written for water up to 25°C, applies a de-rating factor between 25°C and 45°C, and uses classes PN 6, PN 8, PN 10, PN 12.5, PN 16, PN 20 and PN 25. The numbers line up loosely in bar, but the reference conditions behind them do not.

If a tender says PN 10, ask whether it means ISO PN 10 or IS Class 5. On a hot-climate site that question is worth asking before the order, not after.

SWR pipe — IS 13592

Soil, waste and rainwater pipe. Also sized by outside diameter in mm, 40 mm to 315 mm. Two types, and they are not substitutes:

  • Type A — ventilation pipework, rainwater and rainwater harvesting

Note that Schedule 40 and Schedule 80 share the same OD at every size. The schedule changes the wall, and therefore the bore and the pressure rating — not the outside. That is why the same fitting socket accepts both.

HDPE — IS 4984

HDPE is sized by outside diameter too, from 20 mm up to 400 mm in the common range, but the class system is different again: material grade (PE 63, PE 80, PE 100) combined with SDR, giving PN ratings from roughly PN 2 to PN 20 depending on grade.

Two pipes of the same OD and the same PN can have different walls if the grades differ. Specify grade, OD and PN together — never PN alone.

What to put on the purchase order

Five lines, and sizing arguments stop:

  1. Standard number — IS 4985, IS 13592, IS 15778, IS 4984, ASTM D1785
  2. Outside diameter in mm — not the inch name
  3. Class, PN, SDR or schedule, and for CPVC whether CTS or IPS
  4. Length and end type — plain, self-socket, ring-fit
  5. Quantity in metres, not in pipes

Everything you need to check a delivery against that order is printed on the pipe itself — manufacturer, OD, class, standard number and batch, repeated at intervals. If the print legend does not match the order, stop before the pipe goes into the ground.

What to take away

  • Plastic pipe is named by outside diameter; steel and GI by nominal bore — they never line up
  • "1 inch" is 28.6 mm in CTS, 33.4 mm in Schedule, and does not exist at all in IS 4985
  • Pressure class changes the wall and the bore, never the outside — which is why fittings interchange and flow does not
  • IS Class and ISO PN are written at different reference temperatures; do not convert one into the other
  • Put the standard, the OD in mm and the class on the purchase order and the argument disappears

Getting the right pipe on the container

Most of the sizing errors we see on export enquiries are inherited: a consultant's specification written in inches, a contractor's bill of quantities written in mm, and a supplier quoting whichever system is convenient. Common questions

Is PVC pipe measured by inside or outside diameter?

By outside diameter. All the plastics standards used in India — IS 4985, IS 13592, IS 15778, IS 4984 — name the pipe by its OD in millimetres. The wall thickens inwards as the pressure class rises, so the bore shrinks while the outside stays the same.

What is the outside diameter of 1 inch PVC pipe?

It depends on the system. CPVC in CTS sizing is 28.6 mm. PVC or CPVC in Schedule 40 or 80 is 33.4 mm. IS 4985 uPVC has no 1 inch size at all — the nearest is 32 mm. Always specify the standard alongside the size.

What is the difference between nominal bore and outside diameter?

Nominal bore names an approximate inside dimension and is used for steel and GI pipe. Outside diameter names the actual outside and is used for all plastic pipe. A 25 mm nominal bore GI pipe measures about 33.7 mm across the outside.

What sizes does SWR pipe come in?

IS 13592 covers 40 mm to 315 mm outside diameter. The common sizes are 75, 90, 110 and 160 mm, still called 2½, 3, 4 and 6 inch in the trade. Type A is for ventilation and rainwater, Type B for soil and waste.

Can I use CTS CPVC fittings on Schedule 80 CPVC pipe?

No. They are different diameter systems. A 1 inch CTS fitting has a 28.6 mm socket and a 1 inch Schedule 80 pipe is 33.4 mm across. Decide CTS or Schedule 80 at enquiry stage.

What do the IS 4985 pressure classes mean?

They are working pressures at 27°C: Class 1 is 0.25 MPa, rising to Class 6 at 1.25 MPa. They are not the same as ISO 1452 PN classes, which are written for water up to 25°C with a de-rating factor above that.
