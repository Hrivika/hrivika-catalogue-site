How to import PVC pipes from India without losing money

Two importers can buy the same pipe, from the same factory, at the same price, and land it at very different costs.

2 Aug 2026 6 min read Hrivika export desk

The difference is rarely the price per metre. It is how the pipe was loaded, which Incoterm was agreed, and whether the paperwork was right when the container reached the port.

This is a guide to getting all three right.

Why buy from India at all

India makes an enormous amount of plastic pipe. The domestic market was worth around USD 2.10 billion in 2025 and is forecast to reach USD 3.65 billion by 2034. That demand comes from national water and irrigation programmes, and it has built modern extrusion capacity at scale.

For you as a buyer, that matters for one reason. A supplier competing hard at home cannot quote you a lazy export price.

The sea lanes help too. A container from India's west coast reaches Jebel Ali in about three to five days. East African and South Asian services run frequently. This is a well-worn trade route, not a new one.

Specify by standard, not by description

"Give me a price for 110 mm pipe" will get you a price. It will not get you the right pipe.

Specify the standard. These are the Indian ones:

  • IS 4985 — uPVC pipe for drinking water
  • IS 13592 — SWR drainage pipe (Type A or Type B)
  • IS 15778 — CPVC pipe for hot and cold water
  • IS 4984 — HDPE pipe

If your consultant works to international standards, the equivalents are ISO 1452 or EN 1452 for uPVC, ISO 4427 or EN 12201 for HDPE, and ASTM D1785 or ASTM D2846 for the American system.

Then add four more things to your enquiry:

  1. The pressure class, schedule or SDR
  2. The jointing method — solvent socket, ring-fit or threaded
  3. The size range and quantity per size
  4. The manufacturer's test certificate, sent with the quotation rather than after the order

How to check a supplier

Most disappointments are avoidable. Five questions do the work.

Who actually makes it? A serious sourcing partner will name the factory. If the answer is vague, the sourcing is vague. Hrivika sources from established manufacturers — Supreme Industries for pipes, Zoloto Industries for valves, and others of the same standing.

Can I see the print legend? Ask for a photograph of the marking on the pipe. It should show the standard number, the class, the size and a batch code.

Who handles the conformity certificate? Ask before you order, not after. More on this below.

What exactly is the Incoterm, port, packing and lead time? Get it in writing on the proforma invoice.

Is this a registered exporter? A real company with an Import Export Code and a physical address. Hrivika Global is the export arm of Hindustan Cement Agency, a building-materials business that has been trading for 45 years.

Container loading is where the money is

Freight is charged by the container, not by the kilo. Pipe is light and hollow, so a pipe consignment fills the space long before it reaches the weight limit. How the pipe is stuffed decides your landed cost per metre.

The big lever is telescopic nesting — sliding smaller pipes inside larger ones so you are not paying to ship air.

A 40 ft high-cube gives you close to double the space of a 20 ft container for roughly 60 to 70% more freight. For pipe, the 40 ft is almost always cheaper per metre once your volume justifies it.

Things to specify on the packing:

  • Pipe cut or bundled to fit the container — about 5.8 m in a 20 ft, 11.8 m in a 40 ft
  • Bundles strapped at regular intervals
  • End caps, so the pipe does not go oval under load
  • Fittings boxed and packed inside the larger pipe to use the void
  • Mixed loading — pipe alongside valves and fittings in one container

That last point matters if your order is not big enough to fill a box on pipe alone. Consolidating several product lines into one full container is almost always cheaper than shipping part-loads.

FOB or CIF?

Most trade out of India runs on one of these two.

One point people miss

Under CIF the risk still transfers to you when the goods are loaded in India, even though the seller pays the freight. If something happens at sea, it is your insurance claim.

The paperwork

The standard document set from India:

  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of lading
  • Certificate of origin
  • Shipping bill, filed on the ICEGATE portal
  • Insurance certificate, if the terms are CIF
  • Pre-shipment inspection or conformity certificate, where your country requires it

The invoice, packing list and HS code must agree exactly. A mismatch between the declared quantity and the packing list is one of the most common reasons a consignment gets held.

For PVC pipes and fittings, the HS heading is 3917 — pipe under 3917.23, fittings under 3917.40.

Do not ship without the conformity certificate

Many importing countries run a pre-shipment conformity scheme. Without the certificate, your consignment can be refused entry, fined or sent back — and the certificate cannot be issued after the goods have sailed.

Schemes you are likely to meet include ECAS in the UAE, run by the Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology; pre-shipment verification through QGOSM in Qatar; the KEBS Pre-Export Verification of Conformity in Kenya, per consignment, through an appointed agent such as SGS, Intertek, Bureau Veritas or Cotecna; and SLSI import inspection in Sri Lanka against the relevant SLS standard. Requirements change, so check the current rule for your destination before you book.

Build the inspection window into your schedule from day one. Treat a supplier who cannot answer questions about this as a supplier who has not done it before.

What to take away

  • Specify the standard and the class, not just the diameter
  • Ask who makes it, and ask for the print legend and test certificate before you order
  • Nesting is the difference between shipping pipe and shipping air
  • FOB moves the freight bill to you; neither term moves the risk, which passes in India
  • The conformity certificate cannot be issued once the goods have sailed

Common questions

What HS code covers PVC pipes?

Heading 3917 — pipe under 3917.23, fittings under 3917.40.

How much pipe fits in a 40 ft container?

It depends on nesting. Good telescopic loading fills about 95% of roughly 68–76 cubic metres.

Should I buy FOB or CIF?

FOB if you have your own freight rates. CIF if you want one delivered price.

Do I need a conformity certificate?

Usually yes. Many importing countries run pre-shipment schemes. Check before booking.

What is the minimum order?

Enough to justify a container. A mixed load of pipe, fittings and valves usually gets you there.
